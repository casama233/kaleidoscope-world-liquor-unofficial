# World Liquor 0.1.91 — original bottled completion audio

Cola and tonic water now emit the two official Minecraft1.21.1 completion events inherited by the current NeoForge1.21.1 author1.1.11 `BottledDrinkItem`: Player burp first, then LivingEntity eating, before food effects and the existing original-stack/container settlement. The drink's native use animation/pulses remain separate; no generic food or foreign addon audio controls are replaced.

Original selected samples are imported from Mojang's asset index17: one burp sample and three eating variations. Their original player/neutral categories, volume0.5/1 and16-block event ranges are preserved. Source pitch operations use24-bit nextFloat inputs and float multiplication/subtraction/addition at every JVM operation. Six burp and36 eating combinations are independently executed by the Java oracle and checked against the actual runtime helper. The audio/world RNG input consumes three draws before the separate entity/effect probability input. Both default to script Math.random today; their native shared states are not reproduced or claimed.

Each sound reads fresh actor coordinates while keeping the original finishUsingItem world. A rejected sound transport is bounded diagnostics and does not prevent effects or container settlement. Existing0.1.90 Creative, origin-stack, inventory priority, metadata and full-inventory cases remain intact. Registered callback tests cover source order; these are not simulated players or a human listening test.

Actual client comparison for Dot: Java1.21.1 with current World Liquor1.1.11, and Bedrock with this candidate. Complete one cola and one tonic water in Survival and Creative. Compare the completion burp followed by eating audio against the original use/pulse sounds. Cancel halfway and change selected slots: the two added completion events must not play. Have a second real player listen beside the drinker and across16 blocks; compare category sliders and attenuation. Check that effects and exactly one Survival bottle still settle if audio is disabled.

Remaining exact-parity requirements include native completion/event timing, original food stats/advancements/game events, shared world/entity RNG state, native transport/sample selection and acoustic attenuation, particle/input/player animation, arbitrary source hooks and all previously listed gameplay gaps. Client=false and production_ready=false remain until actual comparison. This release does not claim complete Java parity or final audio acceptance.
